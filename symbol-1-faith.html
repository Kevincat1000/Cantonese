Religious Beliefs and Ancestral Worship

Theme: Spiritual Anchors and the "Temple-Association" Model

Overview

Guangdong immigrants transplanted their spiritual landscape to the U.S. through a unique "Temple-Association" model. This integration of sacred and secular spaces—exemplified by the Tin How Temple in San Francisco (est. 1852)—served as a vital cultural nexus. Beyond worshiping Mazu or Guandi, these sites functioned as social hubs where ancestor worship reinforced lineage identity and community cohesion. By recreating familiar ritual spaces, early immigrants transformed traditional Lingnan folk beliefs into a visible public culture in the West. This "regeneration" allowed the community to maintain its cultural "root" while navigating the challenges of life in a foreign land, ultimately enriching America's multicultural religious landscape through festive performances like lion dances and opera.
