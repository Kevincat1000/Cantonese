Cantonese as Linguistic Capital

Power, Hierarchy, and Identity in the North American Chinese Diaspora

Beyond communication, language is capital. For over a century, Cantonese served as the dominant "currency" of North American Chinatowns, granting access to employment, protection, and social belonging.

1. The Era of Hegemony, 1850s–1970s

Huiguan Power: Associations as Gatekeepers

In 19th and 20th-century Chinatowns, native-place associations (huiguan) acted like a government. They dominated trades, settled disputes, and provided welfare. Because Guangdong native networks deeply shaped these institutions, Cantonese was the price of admission.

Speaking the language functioned as a "survival license." Without it, a Chinese immigrant could not borrow money, find a job, or access protection. The community's support system was effectively closed to anyone who did not speak the language.
