The Era of Hegemony, 1850s–1970s: Cantonese as “Survival License”

Early Cantonese dominance was not just about numbers—it was about institutional power. In the 19th and 20th-century Chinatowns, native-place associations (huiguan) acted like a government. They dominated trades, provided welfare, and fought legal battles against discrimination.

Because Guangdong native networks deeply shaped these institutions, Cantonese was the price of admission. Without it, an immigrant could not borrow money or find a job.

I.W. Taber, “3546 The New Chinese Joss House, SF,” c.1891.
