About Me &
This Project

Hi, I’m Kevin. This project started in a pretty simple way: I really like dim sum. Growing up, it was something I always looked forward to, but over time, I started wondering how these traditions traveled so far from where they began. Visiting Hong Kong last summer made everything feel real to me. It pushed me to look beyond just enjoying the culture and start asking questions about where it comes from.

I started working on this six months ago, and I plan to spend the next year finishing it. I like filming and recording things, so instead of just reading textbooks, I decided to learn through interviews. I’m talking to people from different generations about their families, their languages, and the traditions they keep. My goal is to understand how Cantonese culture moves across places and time through the lens of ordinary routines and everyday stories.

Note

For the purpose of this archive, "Cantonese" denotes the broad diaspora from Guangdong Province. Although ethnolinguistically diverse—comprising groups such as the Hakka and Teochew—the social dynamics of early North American Chinatowns established Cantonese as the dominant lingua franca. Consequently, these communities often assimilated into the Cantonese vernacular for survival and cohesion. Here, the term reflects that collective, historically contingent identity rather than a strictly defined linguistic category.
