About Me &
This Project

Hi, I’m Kevin. This project started in a pretty simple way: I really like dim sum. Growing up, it was something I always looked forward to, but over time, I started wondering how these traditions traveled so far from where they began. Visiting Hong Kong last summer made everything feel real to me. It pushed me to look beyond just enjoying the culture and start asking questions about where it comes from.

I started working on this six months ago, and I plan to conduct further interviews on an ongoing basis. I like filming and recording things, so instead of just reading textbooks, I decided to learn through interviews. I’m talking to people from different generations about their families, their languages, and the traditions they keep. My goal is to understand how Cantonese culture moves across places and time through the lens of ordinary routines and everyday stories.

Note

In this project, the term 'Cantonese' is employed in a broad geographic and historical sense to denote migrants from Guangdong Province and their descendants across the diaspora. While diverse groups like the Hakka and Teochew existed, the social dynamics of early Chinatowns established Cantonese as the dominant lingua franca, compelling these communities to adopt the vernacular for survival. Thus, 'Cantonese' here reflects a collective historical identity rather than a strict linguistic category.
