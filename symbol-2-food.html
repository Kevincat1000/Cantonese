Symbol 2: Culinary Heritage and the Global Table

Theme: From Survival Strategy to Global Linguistic Influence

The evolution of Cantonese cuisine in the U.S. marks a remarkable journey from "survival" to "cultural dominance." From only five restaurants in 1856 to over 50,000 today, the proliferation of Cantonese staples such as Chow Mein and Fried Rice provided immigrants with a critical economic foothold during the Exclusion Era. These dishes bridged the gap between Chinese kitchens and Western palates, becoming the foundation of the global "Chinese takeout" culture. More importantly, this dissemination led to the linguistic integration of Cantonese terms—not only "Chow Mein" but also "Dim Sum" and "Won Ton"—into the global lexicon. Yum Cha (dim sum) further evolved into a cross-cultural "scene of public life," showcasing how Cantonese food culture "regenerated" from a marginalized community's sustenance into a ubiquitous symbol of cosmopolitan urban life.
